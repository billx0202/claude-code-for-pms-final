Part 1 · August

What happened, in plain words

We gave responders less time to answer, then punished them for not answering in time.

⏱️

1. Less time to answer

When a job comes in, Rook buzzes a responder's phone. In August's update, 4.2, we cut the time they have to answer from 90 seconds to 60.

📵

2. More jobs slipped past

Overnight, the share of jobs nobody answered in time jumped from about 1 in 40 to nearly 1 in 5.

📉

3. Treated as a "no", forever

Rook counted a slow answer exactly like saying no. Each one pushed the responder down the queue, and nothing ever let them climb back.

1 in 5jobs went unanswered in time after the update (was 1 in 40)
12 → 0jobs a week offered to Farlight, from July to the end of August
4responders pushed out of their own areas, while neighbours took their work
45complaints after the update, and none before it

It wasn't the usual summer slowdown. Summer explains fewer jobs overall, not people missing them, and September brought no recovery.

Why it got worse, not better

A trap with no way out

Every missed buzz moved a responder further back in the queue. Further back meant fewer jobs, and fewer jobs meant fewer chances to win their place back.

It wasn't that these four were slower or further away. Before the update they were as busy as anyone. The scoring rule alone explains who got pushed out.

What we found

4X-Ray Vision · looking inside

The rulebook said one thing. The software did another.

Our own glossary says a missed buzz is "recorded separately from turned down". Inside the software, it's the same thing:

+8points for taking a job
−12points for saying no
−12points for being too slow, the same as a no
# the routing code, unchanged since 2019
def record_declined(responder):
    """They turned it down, or we ran out of time waiting.
    Score goes down. Same either way ..."""
In plain English: one slow answer wipes out one and a half good ones, and the score never heals by itself. An engineer asked in 2019 whether it should, and nobody answered.

How we know

Part 4 · From now on

How we avoid this in future

Six things went wrong. Each one has a habit that prevents it.

Nobody said what "working" looked like

Decide how we'll know it worked, before we ship

The change to buzz time had no success measure, so nobody watched missed jobs after launch.

Checklist ②
The owner left nine days later

Name an owner who stays through the review

The person who owned 4.2's changes left on 21 Aug, and the follow-up had nobody.

Checklist ①
The fix came before the problem

Say who could get hurt, before what we'll build

The roadmap entry said "tune how long a ping waits" and never said why, or for whom.

Checklist ④
Two changes shipped at once

Keep scope tight, and change one thing at a time

Buzz time and queue order changed in the same release, which made the cause hard to see.

Checklist ③
Nobody could see the silence

Make problems show up on screen, not in complaints

Handlers had no explanation, and only 1 miss in 7 was ever reported. The fix adds the explanation and a two-day alert.

Built into the fix
A guess was treated as an answer

Check the numbers before deciding

"It's seasonal" was a guess made before anyone looked. September proved it wrong. Open questions, like the one from 2019, get answered before launch.

How we'll handle it, step by step

Three decisions, four weeks to prove it, and a habit that stays

  1. NowHelen: ship early, or with the next update?Waiting keeps the four shut out. If we wait, we can restore their place in the queue first.
  2. This week30 minutes with Wen and MarcusAgree how much a miss should cost, and how fast a score heals. Low scores heal; good ones are never pulled down.
  3. Before buildCheck two factsThe four's real scores before 12 Aug, and whether some buzzes arrived late on the phone.
  4. LaunchShip the fix, and tell peopleRestore the four's place. Handlers see why someone's quiet and get a two-day alert. Phones explain a missed buzz. Support answers the 45 complaints.
  5. +4 weeksDid it work?All four back to at least 6 jobs a week, misses reported on their own, no new "phone never goes off" complaints. Then decide the buzz time.
  6. Every timeRun the review checklist on every briefOwner, success measure, scope, problem first. Nothing ships without all four.
