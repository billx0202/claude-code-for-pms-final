Missed isn't No

What we'd build instead of changing a number, seen by the people it happens to.

Problem

Since 4.2 (12 Aug), a ping waits 60 seconds instead of 90. Routing priority scores a missed ping exactly like a turned down one, and the score never recovers by itself. A responder who misses a few pings drops in the order, so they get fewer pings, which means fewer chances to take one and climb back.

2.3% → 18%pings missed, before vs after release day
−0.12 vs +0.08what a miss costs vs what a take earns back
~12 → 3–4pings a week for Farlight, The Undertow, Meteor Mite, Vesper

Farlight got no pings at all from 28 Aug onward, while 16 Uptown callouts went to neighbours. Nobody could see it happening. Two of the four handlers never filed a ticket.

The glossary already says missed is "recorded separately from turned down". The code doesn't do that. Wen Li's 2019 note beside it asks whether a score should "ease back" on its own, and nobody answered.

Meteor Mite

What changes for them

1

A missed ping stops counting as a no.

A miss gets recorded and scored as its own outcome, with a much smaller penalty than a turn-down. Low scores also climb back toward neutral over a few weeks, which answers Wen's 2019 question with a yes. Recovery only ever lifts a low score; it never pulls a good one down (see the replay below). Someone who had a bad week of thumbs-on-a-gone-screen isn't still paying for it in October.

2

Kip's coverage card says why it's quiet.

When a responder's pings drop well below their usual week, the card shows one plain line. It links to that responder's recent pings (taken, turned down or missed, with the time of each).

Console · Coverage card
Meteor Mite: 3 pings this week (usually ~12). 9 of the last 14 were missed, not turned down. Mite is being offered later in the order in their area. See recent pings

Kip can now tell Mite something true, like "you're not forgotten, you're missing them, check your notifications". It also explains the other card: The Gale is busy because Mite is quiet.

3

Kip gets an alert after two days in a row with no pings.

If Meteor Mite gets no pings for two days running, while Eastgate had callouts she could have been offered, Kip's console flags it. Her card also shows her pings day by day, so the quiet days stand out.

Console · No-ping alert
Tue 25 Aug: no pings for Meteor Mite for 3 days in a row (since Sun 23 Aug), while 7 Eastgate callouts went out.

We recommend two days, not one. In her real data, a one-day rule would have fired three times in the two normal weeks before 4.2 (29 Jul, 31 Jul, 9 Aug), when The Gale simply got there first. The two-day rule never fires before 4.2, and from 24 Aug it catches every stretch of two or more quiet days. It is a fixed rule, not a setting.

4

Mite's phone tells Mite what happened.

Responder app
You missed a ping at 23:14. It went to another responder. Missed pings don't count against you the way turning one down does.
Several pings missed recently. Check that notifications are on for Rook.

Silence no longer means "something's broken" or "they've dropped me".

5

One-time catch-up for the four.

At launch, the four affected responders go back to where their scores stood before 12 Aug, so they don't have to earn their way out of a hole we dug.

What it would have changed

We replayed each of the four responders' routing scores ping by ping, then simulated every callout in their own area from 12 Aug as if the fix had shipped with 4.2. How often a responder gets asked at each score is measured from the real data. Run under 4.2's own rules, the same replay reproduces the real drop. For Meteor Mite it gives 7.6 → 3.8 → 1.6 → 1.4 pings a week, against an actual 8 → 3 → 1 → 0.

The low end assumes they keep missing as often as they did after 4.2 (53–64% of pings). The high end assumes they answer like a typical responder after 4.2 (18% missed). The replay used a miss penalty of 0.03 and recovery of 5% of the gap a day. Those are illustrative values; Wen sets the real ones.

Recovery must only lift. Easing every score toward neutral would also pull healthy scores down, and in the replay that cost pings rather than restoring them.

The fix is necessary but not sufficient. If the four keep missing 6 pings in 10, the fix roughly halves the drop instead of preventing it. That makes the ping wait and the late-notification question more urgent, not less.

What it deliberately doesn't do

  • Change the ping wait. 60 vs 90 seconds is a separate decision, made once Ravi Menon's numbers show what's left.
  • Revert the proximity change. The data shows proximity isn't the cause, and it was long requested.
  • Add a setting or a knob. Nobody sees a raw routing-priority number, and there's no rank or leaderboard.
  • Name who took the callout on the responder's phone. It just says "another responder".
  • Touch the Responder Availability Record, so nothing lands in Supply unannounced.
  • Assume or reconstruct legal identity (Security Policy 4.1). It uses callout history only.
  • Fix late notifications. That's a separate fix; the nudge only points to it.
  • Cover other asks: Availability Confidence, Shared cover between responders, dark mode and per-responder alert sounds are all out of scope.
